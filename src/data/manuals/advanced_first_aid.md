# Advanced First Aid for Disaster Response

## Triage Protocols
### START Triage (Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment)
1. Walking wounded → GREEN (minimal)
2. Assess breathing:
   - Not breathing after airway opened → BLACK (deceased)
   - Respiratory rate >30/min or <10/min → RED (immediate)
3. Assess perfusion:
   - No radial pulse or capillary refill >2s → RED (immediate)
4. Assess mental status:
   - Doesn't follow commands → RED (immediate)
5. Otherwise → YELLOW (delayed)

### JumpSTART (Pediatric Triage, age <8)
- Same as START but account for developmental differences
- If not breathing: check pulse, give 5 rescue breaths first
- Respiratory rate: <15 or >45 → RED
- AVPU response: if not alert, not responding to voice → check pain response

## Massive Hemorrhage Control
### Tourniquet Application
- Apply 2-3 inches ABOVE wound (not over joint)
- Tighten until bleeding stops (may cause extreme pain)
- Note time of application
- Do NOT remove once applied
- Apply second tourniquet side-by-side if bleeding continues

### Wound Packing (Junctional wounds)
- Pack wound tightly with hemostatic gauze (QuikClot/Celox)
- Apply direct pressure for 3 full minutes
- If no hemostatic gauze: use clean cloth, pack tightly
- Apply pressure bandage over packed wound

### Pressure Points
- Brachial artery: inner arm between biceps and triceps
- Femoral artery: groin crease, mid-point between hip and pubic bone
- Apply firm pressure with fingers or fist

## Splinting Fractures
- Splint in position found (do NOT attempt to realign)
- Immobilize joint above AND below fracture
- Pad splint with soft material
- Check circulation (pulse, sensation, movement) before and after
- For open fractures: cover wound with sterile dressing first

## Hypothermia Management
### Stages
- Mild (32-35°C): shivering, confusion, loss of fine motor
- Moderate (28-32°C): shivering stops, drowsy, slow pulse
- Severe (<28°C): unconscious, no reflex, very slow breathing

### Treatment
- Remove wet clothing, insulate from ground
- Warm gradually - do NOT rub extremities (causes cardiac arrest)
- Apply warm packs to: armpits, chest, groin
- Give warm fluids (if conscious and able to swallow)
- Severe hypothermia: handle gently, transport carefully

## Shock Management
### Signs
- Pale, cold, clammy skin
- Rapid weak pulse
- Rapid shallow breathing
- Confusion or unconsciousness
- Thirst, nausea

### Treatment
- Lay patient flat, elevate legs 12 inches (if no spinal injury)
- Keep warm with blanket/jacket
- Do NOT give food/water if abdominal injury suspected
- Monitor ABCs
- Treat underlying cause (bleeding, dehydration, etc.)

## Spinal Injury Management
- Suspect with: falls >10ft, MVC, diving, head trauma
- Maintain inline stabilization (hold head/neck still)
- Do NOT move patient unless scene is unsafe (fire, flooding)
- Log roll as single unit if repositioning needed
- Transport on rigid surface (door, board, table)
