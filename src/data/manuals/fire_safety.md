# Fire Safety & Response

## Before Fire (Preparedness)
- Install smoke detectors on every level, test monthly
- Create home fire escape plan with 2 ways out per room
- Designate outdoor meeting point
- Keep fire extinguisher in kitchen (Type ABC)
- Know how to use PASS: Pull, Aim, Squeeze, Sweep
- Learn stop, drop, and roll

## During Fire
### If trapped in building:
- Stay low under smoke (crawl on hands and knees)
- Cover mouth and nose with wet cloth
- Feel doors with back of hand before opening
- If door is hot: do NOT open, seal cracks with wet towels
- Signal at window with cloth/flashlight
- Call emergency services even if you can't speak

### If escaping:
- Get out, stay out - never go back inside
- Close doors behind you to slow fire spread
- Use stairs, NEVER elevators
- If clothes catch fire: STOP, DROP, and ROLL

## After Fire
- Do NOT enter damaged building until cleared
- Watch for structural collapse
- Beware of: hot spots, ash pits, sharp objects
- Document damage for insurance
- Discard food exposed to heat/smoke/soot
- Do NOT use electrical appliances until checked

## Burn Treatment
- Cool burn under running water 10-20 minutes
- Do NOT use ice, butter, toothpaste, or ointments
- Remove jewelry/clothing near burn (before swelling)
- Cover loosely with sterile non-stick dressing
- Seek medical attention for:
  - Burns > 3 inches
  - Deep burns (white/charred)
  - Burns to face, hands, feet, genitals
  - Difficulty breathing

## Smoke Inhalation
- Symptoms: coughing, hoarseness, difficulty breathing, confusion
- Move victim to fresh air immediately
- Monitor breathing, perform CPR if needed
- Seek medical help - internal burns may not be visible

## Wildfire Safety
- Create defensible space: clear vegetation 30ft around structures
- Close all windows, doors, vents
- Fill gutters with water if time permits
- Leave early - don't wait for evacuation order if you feel unsafe
- Keep car full of gas, facing exit direction
- Wear: long pants, long sleeves, cotton/wool (not synthetic)
- Take emergency kit when evacuating
